# Into the Trenches: Predicting Memecoin Pumps Seconds Before They Start

**Ronith Sharmila**, Southwestern Oklahoma State University (ronith.sharmila@swosu.edu)

Preprint in preparation for arXiv (primary: q-fin.TR; cross-lists: cs.LG, q-fin.ST).

## Abstract

Hundreds of thousands of new tokens are created every month on memecoin launchpads such as pump.fun, where creating a tradable token takes only seconds. While most of these tokens die quickly, some undergo a surge, or "pump" in traders' terms: a sudden, short-lived and substantial increase in price. We study whether the onset of a surge can be predicted seconds in advance using only on-chain information that is available at the time of prediction. We construct a point-in-time benchmark with 8.4 million observations of 622,303 pump.fun tokens created between 5 June and 14 July 2026. Surge onset is defined with a prespecified detector, and the final week of data remained unseen until a single, preregistered scoring run. On this held-out week, a gradient-boosted tree model ranks observations with an ROC-AUC of 0.866 in predicting whether a surge will start within the next 60 seconds. Observations in its top 1% contain surge onsets 6.3 times more often than the base rate, with a median lead time of about five seconds. Prediction is harder among actively trading tokens, where the ROC-AUC is 0.768; this is substantially lower than 0.866 but still well above chance. The choice of model matters little: an L2-regularized logistic regression on the same 124 causal features performs similarly, while a 10-million-parameter multimodal Transformer with self-supervised pretraining performs worse. We also caution that predictability does not imply profitability: during the validation week, tokens with scores in the top 1% had a median return of -40% over the next five minutes. Finally, we document how future information leaking through a cost-saving sampling of transactions inflated ROC-AUC from 0.835 to 0.917 on a subset of the data, and we describe the checks that exposed it.
